What this is

A playa gift from Joe Che at Muse Cafe, 8:15 & E. A quick, experimental MVP: built in a few days, given away, open source.

The official Who What Where is chaos. It is overwhelming, goes out of date, does not know your taste, and misses the lineups that camps actually publish on Instagram and their own websites. This guide fixes that.

The verified numbers behind this guide:

  • 3,875 events in the guide. The official listings have 3,465.
  • 1,406 named performers. The official listings name almost none of them.
  • 410 music sets pulled from sources outside the official listings.
  • 38 official listings enriched with a lineup the official entry did not carry.
  • 3,620 events resolved to a real BRC address.
  • Distances computed from official 2026 city geometry.
  • Works with no signal. The whole thing is in the page.

Two concrete examples:

  • The official listing for Camp Mystic's psychedelic session says only "Psychedelic Healing & Transformation / With MDMA". The camp's own site names the speaker: Rick Doblin, who founded MAPS. Same slot, one source names him, the official guide does not.
  • Opulent Temple's "Still Burning" appears in the official listings with no lineup. Here it carries Emanate, Vanjee, Alex Amaro and Richard Mixon.

The official guide does not carry the actual lineups. Camp Mystic publishes 38 named speakers and DJs on its own site, campmystic.org, across its 49 events here; none of those names appear in the official listing. Opulent Temple's DJ sets, Syd Gris and all, reached us through the Rock Star Librarian guide and the Playa Set Library, both published in the last few days. We fold those in and mark where each came from, so every event shows its source.

How to use it

Search and the list are the main way in: type what you want or where you stand, get a ranked list. The map is there when you want it, as an option, not the front door.

1Tell it where you are: your camp, or wherever you're standing. Use BRC addresses like 7:30 & E.
2Pick how you get around: walking, bike, or ebike. Distances recalculate for your speed.
3Search terms or filter by day and category, then sort by distance. Nearest things first.

How it learns what you like

This page gives you proximity and search. Your own AI gives you taste.

Set where you are camped and how you get around using the location box near the top of the Find page. Stored in your browser: no account, no server, nothing sent anywhere. It reorders everything by how far away it actually is at your speed. To be honest, this is location preference, not deep personalisation.

Level 2: install it into your own AI

The repo is open source. Point your own AI assistant or second brain at it and it reads the same event data, but now with everything it already knows about you: the artists in your Spotify library, the talks you have saved, the people you follow, what you told it you are looking for this year.

It can then answer "I am at 7:30 and F, what should I do in the next two hours" against your actual taste, not a generic filter.

What is required: you run it yourself. It is your data, it stays with you, and it costs nothing to whoever hosts this page because the page itself runs no AI.

How this works

No server, no app, no account. Everything you see is inside the page you're reading.

When you load this guide, all 3,875 events, 1,217 camps and the city's street geometry come down with it, about 900KB, roughly one photo. After that it never talks to the internet again. Type an address and the distance is worked out on your phone, from real survey data, instantly. That's why it still works when you're standing in deep playa with no bars.

It also means it costs nothing to run and nothing to give away. There's no backend to pay for, no API to rate-limit you, no analytics watching what you search.

Ingested sources

This guide is gathered and cross-referenced from these sources. Credit to the people and projects behind them:

  • Burning Man official 2026 event listings and camp data: the base 3,465 events.
  • Burning Man official 2026 GIS data: the city geometry every distance is computed from (github.com/burningmantech/innovate-GIS-data).
  • PlayaMap: current 2026 camp addresses (playamap.org).
  • Rock Star Librarian Music Guide 2026: by Kate Houston. 400 events and 1,604 DJ sets with real set times. Data pipeline by Damian Tarnawsky, who builds the Dust app (open source, MIT, dust.events), and Avi Flombaum (rslmusicguide.com). Kate asks only that people print copies and gift them on playa, and supports a GoFundMe for clean water wells in Ghana.
  • Playa Set Library: 9 venues and 236 sets, hand transcribed from Instagram flyers by its author (playasetlibrary.com).
  • Individual camp websites: where speaker names actually live.
  • Camp Instagram accounts: where most schedules are posted as images.

These are other people's work, gathered and cross referenced. If you maintain one of these and want it changed or removed, say so and it will be.

Provenance and trust tiers

Every event displays a visible provenance badge so you can see where a listing came from and how much to trust it:

  • Confirmed: means the source that runs the event published it, such as Burning Man official listings, the camp's own website, or Rock Star Librarian.
  • Reported: means someone else saw it and passed it on, such as a camp Instagram post or handwritten flyer.
  • Unverified: means no source was recorded, or the listing is a running-order entry with no clock time.

Times change on playa regardless of tier, so verify on the day.

What we learned doing it

Four things that surprised us, and that anyone building on this should know:

1The official listings strip the speaker's name. Camp Mystic's own site says Rick Doblin is giving a talk. The official entry for the exact same slot says only "With MDMA".
2Whole series can be invisible. Palenque Norte runs 31 sessions at Camp Soft Landing. Not one appears in the official 2026 data.
3Schedules hide on subpages. Crawling camp homepages found 2 schedules. Crawling /events and /schedule found 11.
4Camps post schedules as pictures. The caption says "pick your portal". The whole week's grid is in the image.

So anything tagged camp site only or from instagram came from a camp's own channel. That makes it the most useful information here and the least verified. Trust it, but check on the day.

Honest limitations

  • About 4% of events have no resolvable address, usually art cars and roaming things.
  • Camp-authored times change on playa. Constantly.
  • Distances are straight lines. The playa is mostly flat and open, so it's close, but deep playa at night with a headwind is its own thing.
  • This was built in a few days as an experiment. It will have mistakes.
