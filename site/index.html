Why this exists

The official Who What Where is chaos. It is overwhelming, goes out of date, does not know your taste, and misses the lineups that camps actually publish on Instagram and their own websites. This guide fixes that.

The verified numbers behind this guide:

  • 3,875 events in the guide. The official listings have 3,465.
  • 1,406 named performers. The official listings name almost none of them.
  • 410 music sets pulled from sources outside the official listings.
  • 38 official listings enriched with a lineup the official entry did not carry.
  • 3,620 events resolved to a real BRC address.
  • Distances computed from official 2026 city geometry.
  • Works with no signal. The whole thing is in the page.

Two concrete examples:

  • The official listing for Camp Mystic's psychedelic session says only "Psychedelic Healing & Transformation / With MDMA". The camp's own site names the speaker: Rick Doblin, who founded MAPS. Same slot, one source names him, the official guide does not.
  • Opulent Temple's "Still Burning" appears in the official listings with no lineup. Here it carries Emanate, Vanjee, Alex Amaro and Richard Mixon.

This is a quick experimental MVP built in a few days. Times change on playa. Treat the running orders as running orders, not timetables.

How to use it

Search and the list are the main way to use this guide: type what you want or where you stand, get a ranked list. The map is there when you want it, as an option, not the primary front door.

1Tell it where you are: your camp, or wherever you're standing. Use BRC addresses like 7:30 & E.
2Pick how you get around: walking, bike, or ebike. Distances recalculate for your speed.
3Search terms or filter by day and category, then sort by distance. Nearest things first.

How it learns what you like

This page gives you proximity and search. Your own AI gives you taste.

Level 1: distance and speed (this page)

Set where you are camped and how you get around. Stored in your own browser: no account, no server, nothing sent anywhere. It reorders everything by how far away it actually is at your speed. To be honest, this is location preference, not deep personalisation.

Level 2: install it into your own AI

The repo is open source. Point your own AI assistant or second brain at it and it reads the same event data, but now with everything it already knows about you: the artists in your Spotify library, the talks you have saved, the people you follow, what you told it you are looking for this year.

It can then answer "I am at 7:30 and F, what should I do in the next two hours" against your actual taste, not a generic filter.

What is required: you run it yourself. It is your data, it stays with you, and it costs nothing to whoever hosts this page because the page itself runs no AI.
